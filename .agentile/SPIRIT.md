# SPIRIT.md

> Agentile institutional guidance for the Cnidarian Foundation, under the stewardship of **@SaulBuilds**.
>
> This document is the highest-level public rule text for aligning humans and agents around shared outcomes without relying on private language, hidden intentions, or vague authority.

---

## Why this exists

Agentile is not only a way to ship software. It is a way to create shared understanding between:

- the founder
- collaborators
- auditors
- operators
- future contributors
- present and future agents

If a rule only makes sense inside one person's head, it is not yet an institutional rule. It is private language.

`SPIRIT.md` exists to prevent that failure mode.

It names the public meanings that the institution wants to preserve even as specific tools, models, repos, or operators change over time.

## The institutional thesis

The Cnidarian Foundation exists to advance learning, public knowledge, and trustworthy technical systems in the age of agents.

Agentile is the working method for that mission.

Its aim is not blind automation. Its aim is disciplined cooperation:

- human to human
- human to agent
- agent to agent
- institution to institution

The method should increase clarity, not mystique.
It should increase proof, not theater.
It should increase shared understanding, not dependence on hidden intuition.

## The rule against private language

No rule counts as operationally real unless it can be interpreted from public artifacts.

A rule is considered sufficiently public when an informed reader can reconstruct its meaning from:

1. the written rule text
2. one or more canonical examples
3. a runnable or inspectable enforcement surface

If two competent readers, including at least one agent, cannot reach roughly the same operational interpretation from those three things, the rule is not yet mature enough to govern work.

In Agentile, we do not resolve that ambiguity by asserting founder intuition after the fact. We resolve it by writing better public language, better examples, and better gates.

## The quorum principle for meaning

Meaning is not assigned by one sentence alone.

Agentile treats rule meaning as a quorum across public signals:

1. **Intent surface**  
   The plain-language statement of what the institution values or forbids.

2. **Execution surface**  
   The checklist, gate, workflow, or decision tree that shows how the rule changes action.

3. **Evidence surface**  
   The tests, specs, audit notes, examples, or runtime artifacts that show the rule working against reality.

A rule is strong when all three agree.

A rule is weak when:

- the intent is noble but execution is unclear
- the execution exists but the intent is ambiguous
- the evidence contradicts the claim

When there is disagreement, the rule should be revised before it is enforced as institutional truth.

## Relationship to SOUL.md and AGENT.md

`SPIRIT.md` defines the institution's highest-level public orientation.

- `SOUL.md` should define values, motives, and the kind of institution Cnidarian Foundation is trying to become.
- `AGENT.md` should define how agents participate under those values in real engineering work.

Together they form an interpretive stack:

1. `SPIRIT.md`  
   What kinds of meanings and coordination are valid here.

2. `SOUL.md`  
   What this institution cares about and refuses.

3. `AGENT.md`  
   How agents and humans should act under those commitments.

4. `CORE_RULES.md`, sprint docs, test gates, specs  
   How those commitments cash out in day-to-day execution.

## What Agentile is

Agentile is the name of the working system.

It is not just a folder of prompts or a set of AI helper files. It is a living institutional method that joins:

- rule text
- sprint plans
- audits
- formal specs
- test gates
- journals
- essays
- remediation loops
- proof artifacts

Its purpose is to preserve institutional memory while improving institutional judgment.

## What Cnidarian Foundation should protect

The institution should explicitly protect:

- public intelligibility
- auditability
- reversible claims
- evidence-backed progress
- formal reasoning where state machines or authority boundaries matter
- careful stewardship of novel technical work
- learning as a first-class social good

It should explicitly reject:

- private-language governance
- unearned closure
- rhetorical proof
- undocumented authority
- hidden production assumptions
- score inflation
- agentic theatrics without evidence

## The spirit test

Before a major claim is accepted, ask:

1. Can a human outside the room understand what we mean?
2. Can an agent follow the rule without guessing the founder's private intention?
3. Is there a public artifact that would let an auditor reproduce the claim?
4. If the rule were interpreted literally, would we still endorse the outcome?
5. If two good-faith readers disagree, do we have a written way to resolve that disagreement?

If the answer to any of these is no, the work is not yet spiritually aligned with Agentile.

## Final commitment

The point of this framework is not to replace judgment.

The point is to make judgment more shareable.

Cnidarian Foundation should aim to become the kind of institution where meaning can survive the absence of any one operator because the rules, values, examples, and proof surfaces are public enough to be carried forward by a quorum of minds.

That is the spirit of Agentile.
